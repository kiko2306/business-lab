# Data protection position

The stance behind every client engagement (P11, §84.5) — what role Business
Lab plays in a client's data, who holds the keys, and what's promised if
something breaks. This is the policy; turning it into signed contract
language (a DPA clause, a support agreement) is a separate legal step this
doc informs but doesn't replace.

## Controller vs processor

**The client is the controller of their own business data.** It's their
box: they decide what apps run, what gets entered into them, and who has
access. Business Lab doesn't operate a central server that holds or
aggregates any client's data — each deployment is a fully separate install
on hardware the client owns, per [the resale model](../README.md#what-it-is-and-how-its-sold).

**Business Lab is the client's processor** for whatever it touches doing
setup, support and maintenance — installing apps, applying updates,
troubleshooting a backup, helping recover an account. That's an ordinary,
narrow processor relationship (the same shape as any IT support contractor),
formalized as a short Data Processing Agreement alongside the service
contract — not a novel position this project invented.

**Social media tokens are a non-issue, not an exception** (§84.7): each
client's social accounts are authorized through *their own* developer app
under *their own* business registration, staying on their own box. Business
Lab never holds a token, so there's no processor relationship for that data
at all — worth stating explicitly since an earlier draft (§84.3a) assumed
the opposite before this was decided.

## Backup key custody

**The client holds their own Kopia repository passphrase. Business Lab
never sees it, never stores a copy.**

What that means in practice:

- The passphrase is generated by the dashboard and shown once, in the
  Backup settings panel (same as every other generated secret —
  write-only after that, per `docs/app-credentials.md`'s convention).
  It's the client's responsibility to record it somewhere durable —
  a password manager, a printed copy in a safe, whatever fits how they
  already handle sensitive credentials.
- **If it's lost, the backups are unrecoverable.** Not a bug, not a gap to
  close — it's the direct consequence of never holding the key. Say this
  plainly during onboarding, not as fine print discovered during a real
  disaster.
- The trade-off taken deliberately: this is the purest liability position
  available. Business Lab is never a custodian of the means to decrypt a
  client's data, which is a clean, easy-to-explain answer to "who can read
  our backups" (nobody but the client) and keeps the processor relationship
  as narrow as it can be. The cost is a support conversation that has to end
  with "there's nothing I can do" in the specific case of a lost passphrase
  — accepted, not overlooked.

## Disaster recovery — what's actually promised

**Best-effort, no formal SLA.** Backups protect against data loss (a bad
update, a failed disk, a mistake); hardware failure or replacement is
handled on an **informal, best-effort window** — realistically 1–3 business
days to source/rebuild on replacement hardware, not a contracted uptime
guarantee with penalties.

Why this is the right promise for the model, not a corner cut: a committed
RTO/RPO SLA means spare hardware on hand or a cloud failover path kept warm
for every client — real, ongoing operational cost that doesn't match a
solo/small-operation MSP model, and clients at this size (2–15 people, per
[the turnkey build spec](turnkey-build-spec.md)) are choosing this over
enterprise SaaS specifically for cost, not buying enterprise uptime
guarantees. Be honest about that trade at the sales conversation, not after
an incident.

What backups *do* cover, concretely, once [the off-host destination proof
lands](../README.md) (still open — the `disk`/`s3`/`sftp` paths are
code-complete, the live off-host run is pending real hardware): app data,
the database, config — a full rebuild onto new or repaired hardware inside
that same informal window.

## What this doesn't cover

- **The actual DPA/contract clause wording** — needs real legal drafting
  against whatever jurisdiction/contract template the business uses; this
  doc is the stance that clause has to reflect, not the clause itself.
- **P12 — the commercial plan** (BOM pricing, support model, what happens to
  a client's data if they stop paying) — a related but separate decision,
  and one that lands as an Artifact rather than a repo doc since it touches
  pricing (§84.4's public-repo caveat).
- **Licence due diligence** — tracked separately in `docs/licences.md`,
  already current per app.
